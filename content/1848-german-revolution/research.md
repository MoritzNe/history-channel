# Research notes: The German Revolutions of 1848–49

## Key facts

### Background
- The German Confederation (1815–1866) was a loose league of roughly 39 sovereign states, Austria and Prussia among them, with a Federal Diet in Frankfurt. Metternich's system relied on censorship (Carlsbad Decrees).
- Potato blight and harvest failures in the mid-1840s caused hunger and price riots, e.g. the Berlin "Potato Revolution" of April 1847.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_potato_failure
  - https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kartoffelrevolution
- The Hambach Festival of 1832 used black-red-gold as the colours of a liberal, united Germany.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848_in_the_German_states

### Spring 1848
- Paris, 22–24 February 1848: street fighting forces Louis-Philippe to abdicate. Vienna, 13 March: student demonstration, troops fire, Metternich resigns the same day and goes into exile (London, Brighton, Brussels).
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848_in_the_German_states
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klemens_von_Metternich
- 9 March 1848: the Federal Diet adopts black-red-gold as federal colours.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Germany
- Berlin, 18 March 1848: shortly after 14:30 two shots go off on the Schlossplatz, "most probably by accident", hitting no one. Heavy fighting from about 16:30. Church bells ring the alarm across the city. Over 900 barricades. More than 200 civilians killed (the "Märzgefallene") and fewer than 50 soldiers. On 19 March the king gives in, issues "An meine lieben Berliner" and withdraws the troops. Shortly after 14:00 on 19 March the dead are carried to the palace and the crowd forces the king to show respect ("Mütze ab!"). 21 March: the king rides through Berlin with a black-red-gold armband. 22 March: 183 coffins on the steps of the Deutscher Dom, burial in Friedrichshain.
  - https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A4rzrevolution_1848_in_Berlin
- Hecker uprising: Hecker and Struve call armed men out on 12 April 1848. They expected tens of thousands and got a few thousand. 20 April 1848, Scheideck near Kandern: General Friedrich von Gagern, commanding the Baden and Hessian troops, tries to persuade the leaders to surrender and is mortally wounded. Heinrich von Gagern was his younger brother. Hecker flees to Switzerland, then to the USA (farm near Belleville, Illinois).
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Hecker
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Balduin_von_Gagern
  - https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badische_Revolution

### Frankfurt Parliament
- Opened 18 May 1848 in the Paulskirche. Heinrich von Gagern elected president on 19 May (305 of 397 votes). Nicknamed the "Professorenparlament".
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Parliament
  - https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_von_Gagern
- The Germania painting (1848, attributed to Philipp Veit) hung in the Paulskirche. The Commons file is dated 1848.
- Greater German vs. Lesser German question: include Austria with its non-German lands, or a smaller Germany under Prussia. Gagern took the small-German course.
- Basic Rights of the German People, Reich law of 27 December 1848: equality before the law, freedom of the press, of religion and of assembly, the abolition of the death penalty (with exceptions), and more.
  - https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grundrechte_des_deutschen_Volkes
- Constitution proclaimed 28 March 1849 (the Wikipedia intro says it was adopted on 27 March). On 28 March Friedrich Wilhelm IV was elected hereditary emperor with 290 votes and 248 abstentions. The "Note of the Twenty-Eight" (14 April 1849) recorded acceptance by 28 governments.
  - https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiserdeputation
  - https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulskirchenverfassung

### Autumn 1848: counter-revolution
- Robert Blum: born 10 November 1807 in Cologne to a poor family. Theatre servant in Cologne (1830), then theatre secretary in Leipzig (1832). Leader of the left in Frankfurt. Went to Vienna in October 1848 with a Frankfurt delegation and joined the defenders. Arrested 4 November after Windischgrätz took the city. His claim to immunity as a deputy was ignored. Shot 9 November 1848 at about 07:30 in the Brigittenau. Reported last words: "Ich sterbe für die deutsche Freiheit, für die ich gekämpft. Möge das Vaterland meiner eingedenk sein."
  - https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Blum
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Blum
- 10 November 1848: Wrangel's troops march back into Berlin. A state of siege follows on 12 November.
  - https://parlament-berlin.de/Das-Haus/Berliner-Ehrenbuerger/friedrich-heinrich-ernst-graf-von-wrangel
  - https://berlin.kauperts.de/Strassen/Wrangelstrasse-12165-Berlin

### The crown refused
- Friedrich Wilhelm IV to Christian von Bunsen, 13 December 1848 (the "Bunsen letter"), as excerpted by the DHM: "Einen solchen imaginären Reif aus Dreck und Letten gebacken, soll ein legitimer König von Gottes Gnaden und nun gar der König von Preußen sich geben lassen […]?" The same letter speaks of the "Ludergeruch der Revolution von 1848". The DHM glosses "Letten" as "Schlamm" (mud/clay).
  - https://www.dhm.de/assets/DHM/Download/Publikationen/Begleitheft_RNT/1848_Aufgaben_G04.pdf
  - Printed source: L. von Ranke (ed.), *Aus dem Briefwechsel Friedrich Wilhelms IV. mit Bunsen* (1873)
- Related phrases, not used in the script: "Diadem aus Dreck und Letten" (letter to Radowitz, 23 Dec 1848) and "Hundehalsband" (letter to Ernst August of Hanover, April 1849).
- 3 April 1849: the Kaiserdeputation under Eduard Simson is received in the Berlin palace and the king does not accept. 28 April 1849: final written rejection.
  - https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiserdeputation

### 1849: the last stand
- Dresden May Uprising, 3–9 May 1849: Wagner supported the provisional government and fled on 9 May. Bakunin also took part. About 2,500 combatants.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848_in_the_German_states
- Baden: the Rastatt garrison mutinies on 9 May 1849. Grand Duke Leopold flees in the night of 13–14 May. In June, federal troops and two Prussian corps under the Prince of Prussia (Wilhelm, later Emperor Wilhelm I) invade. Rastatt is besieged from 30 June and capitulates on 23 July 1849. Courts-martial (27 July–27 October) carry out 27 death sentences: 19 in Rastatt, 3 in Freiburg, 5 in Mannheim. About 80,000 Badeners (about 5% of the population) emigrated, mainly to America, for economic reasons and partly because of the failed revolution.
  - https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badische_Revolution
- Rump parliament: moved to Stuttgart, presided over by Wilhelm Löwe from 6 June, and broken up by Württemberg troops on 18 June 1849.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Parliament

### Forty-Eighters
- Carl Schurz (born 2 March 1829, so 20 in July 1849) was in Rastatt and escaped through an unguarded sewer, then crossed the Rhine to France. He freed Kinkel from Spandau in 1850 and emigrated to the US in 1852. Union brigadier general (1862), major general (1863), US Senator for Missouri (1869–75), Secretary of the Interior (1877–81).
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Schurz
  - https://journals.ku.edu/ygas/article/download/18285/16434/44889
- Hecker: colonel of the 24th Illinois (1861) and the 82nd Illinois (1862–64), wounded at Chancellorsville.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Hecker

### Legacy
- Weimar Constitution 1919: black-red-gold becomes the national flag, and the Frankfurt basic rights serve as an important model.
- Basic Law 1949: Article 22 reads "The federal flag shall be black, red and gold." Members of the Parlamentarischer Rat quoted the Paulskirche constitution during their work.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Germany
  - https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulskirchenverfassung
- Wilhelm, Prince of Prussia (commander against Baden in 1849), became German Emperor in 1871.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_I,_German_Emperor
