# Research notes: The German Revolutions of 1848–49 (v2, explainer cut)

This builds on `../1848-german-revolution/research.md`. All facts and image files verified there still
apply and are not repeated here. Below are the **new** claims the v2 narration and maps rely on, plus the
map data choices. Retrieved 27 Oct 2026.

`script.json` is generated by `tools/build.py`: narration is written per shot, shot weights come from
each segment's share of the scene's characters, and every `at: "@phrase"` resolves to the fraction of the shot
at which that phrase is spoken (with a 0.03 lead, clamped to 0.05–0.95). Edit `build.py`, not the JSON, then run:

```
python3 -I content/1848-german-revolution-v2/tools/build.py content/1848-german-revolution-v2/script.json
.venv/bin/python content/1848-german-revolution-v2/tools/check.py content/1848-german-revolution-v2/script.json <dir with world_1815.geojson and world_1880.geojson>
```

## Interpretive or hedged on purpose

- "Three reasons" (power, division, geography) in s20 is a synthesis of the standard historiography, framed as explanation rather than quotation.
- "In March the princes lost their nerve, not their armies" is interpretive but well supported: the armies stayed intact and loyal, as Prague, Vienna, Berlin, Dresden and Baden all showed.
- "Driven mostly by poverty, some by politics" (Baden emigration): de.wiki Badische Revolution says mainly economic and partly political.
- The 585/809 figures carry "about" and "around" in the narration and "figures vary" on the stat card.

## Map data

- Basemaps: aourednik/historical-basemaps `world_1815.geojson` (all 1848–49 maps) and `world_1880.geojson` (the 1871 map and the Atlantic maps), GPL-3.0. Every `MapRegion.historical` name was checked against the files' NAME/ABBREVN/SUBJECTO with `tools/check.py`.
- Names used for 1815: Prussia, Austrian Empire, Lombardy, Venetia, Bavaria, Palatinate, Saxony, Hanover, Württemberg, Baden, Grand Duchy of Hesse, Electoral Hesse, Nassau, Thuringia, Mecklenburg-Schwerin, Mecklenburg-Strelitz, Oldenburg, Brunswick, Anhalt, Lippe-Detmold, Schaumburg-Lippe, Waldeck, Holstein, Schleswig, Denmark, Hamburg, Bremen, Lübeck, Hohenzollern, Wetzlar, Cuxhaven, Luxembourg. For 1880: Germany, Austria Hungary.
- The German Confederation is not a basemap polygon, so it is drawn as a hand-built approximate ring (`CONFED` in `build.py`) and labelled "(approx.)" where it is the subject. It includes Holstein, Bohemia, Moravia, Austrian Silesia, Tyrol with Trentino, Carniola, Trieste, Luxembourg and Limburg, and it excludes Schleswig, East and West Prussia and Posen.
- "Lesser Germany" means all 1815 member polygons except the Habsburg lands, labelled "(approx.)". The whole "Prussia" polygon includes East Prussia and Posen, which in 1848 were partly admitted to the Confederation, so this is acceptable at video scale.
- "Accepted the constitution" (s16) uses the clearly documented states: Baden, Württemberg, both Hesses, Nassau, Oldenburg, Brunswick, Thuringia, Holstein, the three Hanseatic cities and Lippe-Detmold. Mecklenburg and Anhalt are left unshaded because their status was not checked.
- Movement arrows marked "(approx.)" are schematic: Hecker's column (Konstanz–Stockach–Engen–Bonndorf–Kandern) and Schurz's escape (Rastatt–Rhine–Alsace–Zürich). The exact crossing point is not verified. Jelačić's arrow starts in western Hungary, also schematic.
- Coordinates used: Rastatt 8.20E 48.86N, Frankfurt 8.68/50.11, Mannheim 8.47/49.49, Konstanz 9.17/47.66, Kandern 7.66/47.71, Germersheim 8.37/49.22, Waghäusel 8.51/49.25, Kaiserslautern 7.77/49.44, Rendsburg 9.67/54.30, Malmö 13.00/55.60, Peterswaldau (Pieszyce) 16.58/50.71, Carlsbad 12.87/50.23, Mainz 8.27/50.00, Weimar 11.33/50.98, Bonn 7.10/50.73. The full list is `C` in `build.py`.
