# Pass 1: Research

Topic: **$topic**
Target video length: about $minutes minutes. Narration language: $language.

Research this topic thoroughly with the web search tool before writing anything. Search broadly first (overview, chronology), then deeply: primary sources, the key people, the disputed points, surprising details, the aftermath, and how historians judge it today. Aim for 8 to 15 distinct, reputable sources. Do not call `submit_script` or `submit_critique` in this pass.

Then write a research dossier in Markdown with these sections:

1. **Summary**: 5 to 8 sentences on what happened and why it matters.
2. **Timeline**: the essential events with dates and places (this is for us, not for the narration).
3. **People**: each key person, who they were, what they wanted, what they did, what happened to them. Include motives and contradictions.
4. **Key moments**: 6 to 12 vivid, concrete moments a scene could be built around, each with the specific details that sources actually record.
5. **Numbers**: every figure that might appear in the video or on a stat card (casualties, sizes, counts, votes, distances, money), each with its range and who reports it.
6. **Disputed or uncertain points**: what historians disagree about or what is legend, with the competing views.
7. **Verified quotations**: exact wording (original language plus English translation if different), speaker, context, date, and source URL. Mark anything apocryphal as such. Only include quotations you saw in a source.
8. **Geography**: every place involved, with coordinates in decimal degrees (longitude, latitude). Include every movement (marches, troop advances, flights, routes with waypoints, migrations) and every territory the story depends on: states, memberships, contested areas, rival border options, and which states sided with whom. Say which period basemap fits (around 1815, around 1880, or modern borders), and note where historical borders must be approximated.
9. **Visual leads**: portraits of each key person, and paintings, engravings, photographs or documents that show what the narration describes (name, artist, date; Commons file titles only if you saw them), plus good Commons search terms.
10. **Modern relevance**: honest, specific connections to today.
11. **Sources**: a numbered list with title, author or publisher, and URL. Cite them as [1], [2], ... in the sections above.

Be precise. Where sources conflict, say so. Where you could not verify something, say so.
