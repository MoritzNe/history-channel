# Pass 2: Outline and packaging

Topic: **$topic**
Target length: about $minutes minutes (about $words spoken words). Narration language: $language.

Using the research dossier above, design the video before a word of narration is written. Do not call `submit_script` or `submit_critique` in this pass; you may use web search to fill a genuine gap.

Return Markdown with these sections:

1. **Central question**: the one question the video exists to answer, phrased the way a curious viewer would ask it. It must be answerable from the research.
2. **Title options**: 10 YouTube titles, each at most 70 characters, specific and curiosity-driven but honest (the video must deliver what the title promises). Vary the angle: mystery, stakes, character, consequence, myth-busting. No clickbait the facts don't support, no ALL CAPS. Mark your top pick with ★ and say why in one line.
3. **Thumbnail concept**: main image (ideally a striking map or a real archival image), 2 to 4 words of overlay text that don't repeat the title, composition and contrast, emotion on faces if any.
4. **Hook**: the first 20 seconds as a short paragraph of intent: the opening map animation (what appears, spreads or moves), and the moment the central question lands.
5. **Act structure**: for each act (hook, setup, escalation, turn, answer, legacy), give its purpose, the scenes in it, the question it raises or answers, and its approximate duration in seconds. Durations must add up to the target length. State the explicit answer to the central question (the numbered reasons) that the answer act will give.
6. **Map plan**: the 8 to 15 key maps in order. For each, give the extent (and `camera_to` if the camera moves), the basemap, and what animates (markers, arrows, regions, stamps) to explain which point. Also list the 5 to 7 events of the recurring timeline, and where it appears.
7. **Characters**: the 2 to 6 people who get a person card, with their role and motive in one line each.
8. **Stat cards**: the 3 to 6 numbers worth a full-screen card, each with its source and any hedge.
9. **What we leave out**: interesting material we cut to keep the story tight, so the draft doesn't sneak it back in.
10. **Risk notes**: disputed figures and claims that must be hedged in the narration, and quotations that are safe to use because they are verified in the dossier.
